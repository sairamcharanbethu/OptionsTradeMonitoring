## Decision 1 — GEX_WALL_BOUNCE disabled ✅ (shipped)

Baseline bounce trades: **n=12, 17% win, mean −$40.42 ± $13.08 SE — ~3.1 SE
below zero**, consistent with the June–August sub-sample measured first
(−$40.99 ± 14.48, n=8; ~+4 independent April–May trades confirmed rather than
reverted). It is the single difference between portfolio PF 0.88 and 1.03.

Mechanical story: the wall gate arms only in strong negative gamma — exactly
the regime where walls break rather than hold. A bounce entry fights the
condition its own gate selects for. The other two wall reactions trade *with*
that logic and were positive on the same sample (rejection +$390.90 on 8,
failed-break +$151.10 on 13).

Shipped as `DISABLED_WALL_STRATEGIES = {"GEX_WALL_BOUNCE"}` in
signal_engine.py (candidate never arms; evaluator unchanged), pinned by
test_wall_strategy_policy.py. Re-enable only via a fresh uw_backtest run.

## Decision 2 — morning-only entry gate REJECTED ❌ (evidence reversed)

The June–August sample suggested entries after 11:00 ET were poison. The full
window says otherwise: morning_only *underperformed* baseline (−$3.49 vs
−$3.03), and baseline's 11:xx bucket was actually positive (+$6.37/trade,
n=18). The 93-trade pattern was noise. **This is the system working as
intended — a change that looked obvious two weeks ago failed out-of-sample
before touching the engine.**

Removing the proven bleeder makes the system **breakeven-to-slightly-positive
gross (+$0.64/trade, PF 1.03) and still ~flat-to-negative net of ~$1.50–3
round-trip commissions.** This change stops a leak; it does not yet establish

## Fidelity correction: T1 premium lock modeled — headline revised down

The sim had modeled only half the live post-T1 protection (stop-to-trigger)
and omitted the premium profit lock (arm +20%, floor +10%). With the full
live exit policy simulated, the corrected best estimate of the system:

| | without lock (previously reported) | **with lock (true live policy)** |
|---|---|---|
| Expectancy | +$0.66/trade | **−$1.03/trade** |
| PF | 1.03 | **0.96** |
| Win rate | 41% | 43% |

The lock changed 6 of 127 trades: rescued 4 small ones (two T1 give-backs,
two flatten bleeds → ~+10% exits) and clipped 2 large T2 runners ($125.85 →
$34.10, $208.95 → $19.50). Paired: **−$1.69/trade ± $1.76 SE (−0.96 SE) — the
lock's cost is statistically indistinguishable from zero**, driven entirely
by two clipped runners. No evidence to change the lock in either direction.

Take-all-at-T1 vs the corrected baseline: +$0.53 ± $2.48 SE — still nothing.

**Conclusions.** (1) The honest current estimate of the live system is
PF 0.96, −$1.03/trade gross — breakeven within ±$6–7 SE, slightly negative
net of commissions. Prior +$0.66 described an exit policy nobody trades.
(2) Every exit-policy variant tested sits within 1 SE of every other: exits
are not where this system's fate is decided. The leverage is in entries and
family selection. (3) Wall-bounce evidence is unaffected (those trades die
pre-T1).

## Confidence calibration test — the score does not predict outcomes ❌

All 127 trades with the engine's confidence_score recorded (live mapping:
≥90 = A+, 70–89 = A; nothing below 70 executes).

| Cut | n | Win % | Mean/trade |
|---|---|---|---|
| A+ (≥90) | 30 | 43% | +$7.12 ± 9.63 |
| A (70–89) | 97 | 43% | −$3.55 ± 6.67 |

A+ − A difference ≈ $10.67 ± ~$11.7 SE (0.9 SE — noise). Pearson
r(confidence, pnl) = **0.066**. Finer buckets are non-monotonic: [70,75) is
the best bucket (+$50, n=9) and [75,80) the worst (−$26, n=29) — a calibrated
score would be monotonic.

The apparent A+ edge is family identity in disguise: 27 of 30 A+ trades are
CONTINUATION. Within MTF_TREND_BREAK, A+ did *worse* (n=3). "Trade A+ only"
would just be "trade CONTINUATION only" with extra steps, and CONTINUATION
alone is +$11.65 ± 10.26 — still ~1.1 SE, unproven.

Implications: (1) no added selectivity or sizing should key off the score
beyond the existing ≥70 floor; (2) the ~33 hand-set scoring weights that
produce this number carry no demonstrated outcome information — treat the
score as descriptive display, not signal quality; (3) the [75,80) bucket's
−2.1 SE is one of five post-hoc buckets (expected by chance ~17% of the
time) — watch-list only, re-test on fresh rolling data before any action.

## Spread-drag bound (--mid-fills): the fill is the whole ballgame

Same 127 trades, fills priced at candle mid instead of crossing the modeled
spread (an upper bound — real limit fills land between):

| | cross the spread (live-realistic) | mid fills (upper bound) |
|---|---|---|
| Expectancy | −$1.03/trade | **+$2.10/trade** |
| PF | 0.96 | **1.10** |

**Total modeled spread drag ≈ $3.13/trade — larger than every strategy
effect measured this weekend except the wall-bounce removal.** The gap
between "slightly losing" and "modestly winning" is decided at the fill,
not the signal. Caveats: the spread model is synthetic (0.6% of premium
+ $0.01), and mid-fills ignore non-fill risk from patient limits.

Actionable: (1) execution tactics on the live path — work the limit toward
mid rather than paying the protected-limit offset immediately; measurable
live via the existing entry-slippage-vs-mid metric (/api/metrics/performance,
paper_orders). (2) Fee audit: SnapTrade/Wealthsimple per-contract option
fees vs routing execution through the already-connected IBKR gateway —
commission delta is pure, unoverfittable expectancy.
